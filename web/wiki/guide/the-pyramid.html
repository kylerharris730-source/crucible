The pyramid

The desert's landmark, its three treasures, and waking the Sun Warden.

West of where you start, the grass gives out and the ground turns to sand. That is the desert, and in the middle of it stands the pyramid: a sandstone mountain you can see from a long way off, with a boss asleep at its heart and three treasures hidden in its wings.

This page covers finding it, getting around inside it, and waking what lives there. The boss half is a spoiler, and it is marked.

Before you go

  • It is hot enough to hurt. The whole desert, surface and shallow rock alike, sits at 60 °C, which is past the point where heat starts costing you health. Out in it without protection you are slowly cooked. Heat resistance answers it, and one good piece is enough: a Steel Helmet or anything better cancels it outright, as do the boss sigils from the Widow's on, and the Sentinel Ankh that Tomb Sentinels sometimes drop. Iron armour and the Brood Mother's sigil only soften it, and pieces do not add up -- the best one you wear is what counts. Cotton, silk and beeswax do not survive out there either. See How heat behaves.
  • It has its own creatures. The Dune Hound, Scarab and Tomb Sentinel live only in the desert. Dune Striders scuttle across the sand at night in bursts; Amberbacks keep advancing through shallow caves. Go with a real weapon — see Fighting back.
  • Inside is dark. The pyramid's halls count as underground, so creatures spawn in them the way they do in caves. Bring light — see The dark is not scenery.

Getting in

Walk west. The pyramid's base is graded level with the sand on both sides, and there is a tall doorway at ground level on each face.

Both doorways lead along a straight corridor to the central hall, the big room in the middle with a ledge on either side. That is where the boss is woken, and you can walk in and out of it freely until you choose to.

The pyramid is built from Sandstone, so it can be dug like any rock. You never have to. Every room below is reachable on foot.

The three treasures

There are three reward pedestals, one in each wing, and no two are near each other. Right-click a pedestal to take what is on it.

  1. The crypt, below the west corridor. Partway along the west corridor the floor falls away into a slope. Follow it down, under the desert itself, to a sealed crypt at the bottom. It holds a Swift Charm.
  2. The gallery treasury, up from the east door. Just inside the east doorway a long ramp climbs away to the west, above the corridor. At the top is the gallery, a corridor that runs most of the pyramid's width. Turn east at the top, back over the ramp, into the treasury. It holds a Whetstone.
  3. The apex, under the peak. Walk the gallery west to its far end, where a second ramp doubles back up to the east. It comes out on a small landing, and a third ramp from there climbs to the last chamber, just under the peak. It holds a Wisp Prism.

The shortcuts

The outer faces are steep but walkable. Two vents come out onto them:

  • The west vent comes out at the far west end of the gallery.
  • The east vent comes out at the landing below the apex.

Climb the outside of the pyramid and you can reach either one without walking the ramps. It is the quick way to the apex, and the quick way out of it.

The Sun Engine

In the middle of the central hall stands the Sun Engine: a stepped shrine with an obelisk, a glowing eye, and a sun disc turning on top. A shaft of light rises from it to the ceiling, so you can pick it out from the far end of the hall, and it lights the room around it.

It is made of Sun Engine, which no tool can break. Walk up close and a small mouse icon appears above the disc, with its right button lit.

Right-click the Sun Engine to summon the Sun Warden. There is no item to craft and nothing is used up. Only one Warden can be awake at a time. The shaft of light goes out and the disc burns red while it is.

The Sun Warden — spoilers

The Sun Warden is a floating pharaoh's head with a sun disc behind it and two detached hands, tied to its shoulders by threads of light. It flies, so the hall's ledges are somewhere to stand, not somewhere to hide.

Kill the hands first

While either hand lives, the Warden's body takes only a quarter of what you deal it. The health bar shows two pips for the hands beside it, and the word ARMOURED while any are lit. The hands are smaller targets, but they come to you. Hit one when it is lying still after an attack.

What it does

Every attack has a warning first. Learn the warnings and the fight is fair.

  • Fan. Its eyes flare white and it fires a spread of sunlight at you. Move sideways, not back.
  • Beam. It stops, a line of light shows where it will start and a dotted arc shows how far it will go, then it sweeps a beam across that arc from its mouth. Get behind a ledge or under the start of the sweep.
  • Slam. A hand rises over you, then locks, shakes, and drops, with a dashed line and a bracket showing where it will land. The impact sends shockwaves both ways along the floor. Jump them.
  • Sweep. A hand sinks to the floor on the far side of you, and arrows on the floor show which way it will go. Then it drags along the ground. Jump it, or stand on a ledge.
  • Clap. Both hands take station on either side of you, with brackets closing in on the spot. Then they slam together and burst. Leave that spot.
  • Scarab call. Its jaw drops, rings appear on the floor beside it, and two Scarabs climb out. It only does this while there are few scarabs around.

It never uses the same attack twice in a row.

When it burns

Once both hands are dead, or its health falls low enough, the disc turns red and the mask cracks. From then on it moves faster, takes full damage, and gains two more attacks:

  • Nova. It rises, a ring closes in on its disc, and it fires two staggered rings of shards in every direction. Stand where the gaps will be, or far enough away for them to spread.
  • Dive. It hangs above you and marks the floor under you with a dashed line and a bracket, then drops straight onto that spot and sends shockwaves out from it. Move off the mark, then punish it while it rises slowly back up.

The reward

Beating it drops the Sun Sigil, and the game records the win. You can wake it again from the Sun Engine whenever you like.

If everyone goes a long way from it — you die and respawn at a bed across the world, say — the Warden leaves, hands and all. That is not a loss and not a win. Walk back and wake it again.
