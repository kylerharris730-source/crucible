Making fire

Start a fire and keep it under control.

Torches give light but no heat. Use a Flint Striker to start your first fire. One is in your starting pack, and you can make a replacement from 4 Stone by hand.

Start a fire

  1. Put a pile of Wood or Coal where you want the fire. A single cell of fuel may burn out before it lights anything else.
  2. Use the Flint Striker on the pile. It heats the fuel enough to ignite it. Stone, dirt, and metal will not light because they do not burn.
  3. Keep other flammable things away. Fire heats nearby cells and can spread to your bed, workbench, or wooden base. Build around a fireplace with stone or another material that does not burn.

Water puts out fire on contact. Use it if a fire starts spreading where you do not want it.

Fuel and heat

What you burn determines how hot the fire gets and what you can smelt. Wood leaves Wood Ember; coal leaves Ember.

Leaves and cotton reach 205 °C because they burn as open fire, but they do not last long. A coal ember bed stays at 185 °C. That is hot enough to melt copper at 165 °C, but it will not melt iron at 190 °C, no matter how long you wait. See Ore into bars for smelting temperatures.

When you are ready to collect more ore, Digging covers mining tools and materials you cannot dig.
