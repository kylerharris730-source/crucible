# Fighting back

The thing nobody guesses: **a multitool does no damage.** It is a chassis with
slots in it. What a tool does lives entirely on the **modules** you put in.

An empty multitool is a stick.

## You will need

- A [Multitool Mk I](../items/multitool-mk-i.html) — 4 Copper + 2 Wood, at the
  bench
- At least one module. The [Shot Module](../items/shot-module.html) is 2 Copper
  + 1 Wood and does 7 damage; the
  [Blast Module](../items/blast-module.html) does 24

## Steps

1. **Make the multitool.** Mk I holds three modules; Mk II holds five and is
   quicker off the mark.
2. **Put modules in it.** This is the step that turns it into a weapon.
3. **Order matters.** Installed shot modules fire left to right, and modifier
   modules change the spell in front of them rather than firing anything of
   their own.
4. **Upgrade the modules before the chassis.** A Mk II full of nothing is worse
   than a Mk I with a shot module in it.

## When it works

You hold the tool, you click, and something takes damage. The
[Items](../items/index.html) index sorted by the Damage column shows every
module in the game against every other.

Your starting [Bolt Caster](../items/bolt-caster.html) does 4 damage against the
Shot Module's 7, and that gap is the point: it exists so a rock mite is a fight
rather than an execution, and it is weak enough that building a real weapon is
the first thing you want.

## When it does not

**You made a multitool and it does nothing.** It has no modules in it. This is
the single most common confusion in the game and there is nothing in the tool's
appearance to suggest it.

**Your damage did not go up when you upgraded the chassis.** It would not. The
chassis buys slots and firing speed. Damage is on the modules.

**You ran out of charge.** A multitool stores charge and recovers it slowly —
Mk I holds 120 and recovers 1 per frame. Each shot costs some. The numbers are
on each tool's page.

**Something keeps its distance and shoots you.** Some creatures do exactly that,
and the creature page says so — look for "Keeps its distance" and the shot
damage. Closing is not always the answer; those are the ones worth a blast
module.

**A boss appeared and killed you.** Bosses are summoned deliberately and never
wander into you — so if you met one, you called it. That is the design: you
arrive having chosen the ground and the moment, rather than meeting one in a
tunnel with a full pack of ore.

**Armour did not seem to help.** Armour is subtracted per hit, and unlike most
bonuses in this game **a helmet and a suit do add together**. Check you have
both. See [Gearing up](gearing-up.html).
